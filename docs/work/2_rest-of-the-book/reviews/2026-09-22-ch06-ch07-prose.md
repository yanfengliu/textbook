## Errors (false or misleading)

### 1. Chapter 6 §6.6 (`calvin`) — the six-carbon intermediate has been isolated, and its instability is the enzyme's doing

`biology/ch06-photosynthesis/index.html` line 283:

> "the six-carbon compound is so unstable that it splits within the active site, before anything can be done to it, into two three-carbon molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate. Nobody has ever isolated the six-carbon intermediate from a working enzyme."

What is wrong: the intermediate — 2′-carboxy-3-keto-D-arabinitol 1,5-bisphosphate — was prepared by quenching the working enzyme with acid about 12 ms into the reaction and characterised at length; off the enzyme it is not fleeting at all, decarboxylating with a half-life of about an hour over pH 4–11. What is fast is what the enzyme does to it: hydration and cleavage into two 3-phosphoglycerate within milliseconds. So "so unstable that it splits" puts the instability in the wrong place, and "nobody has ever isolated" is simply false. The historical point survives: Calvin's group never saw it among the labelled compounds, because it never leaves the enzyme.

Evidence: Lorimer, Andrews, Pierce & Schloss, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 313:397 (1986), "2′-carboxy-3-keto-D-arabinitol 1,5-bisphosphate, the six-carbon intermediate of the ribulose bisphosphate carboxylase reaction" — https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.1986.0046 (prepared "by quenching the reaction with acid after a short time (ca 12 ms)"; "slow (t½ = 1 h), pH-independent decarboxylation"). The first isolation is Schloss & Lorimer, *J. Biol. Chem.* 257:4691 (1982).

Fix: "the six-carbon compound never leaves the enzyme: within a few milliseconds the active site hydrates it and cuts it in two, into two three-carbon molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate. Calvin's group never found it among the labelled compounds, and catching it took stopping the enzyme with acid twelve thousandths of a second after it started." The key idea's "splits at once" can stand. `FIGURES.md` 6.6 must follow: the line "stating that it has never been isolated from a working enzyme" becomes "stating that it never leaves the enzyme, and what it took to catch it", and the field `sixCarbonIsolated` ("false in every reachable state") should be renamed to something that is true, e.g. `sixCarbonLeftEnzyme`.

Severity: **error**. Objective: `carboxylation-product` — statement unchanged (the three-carbons reason, "cut before it can leave", survives); an item must not test or reward "never isolated". Figure field changes.

### 2. Chapter 6 §6.3 (`pigments`) and the `diagnose` sort — the shade-to-sun lag is rubisco's, not the antenna's, and a graded card is built on the wrong mechanism

`index.html` line 175:

> "On top of that a leaf in bright light deliberately detunes part of its own antenna so that it sheds energy as heat instead of passing it on, which is why a plant moved suddenly from shade into full sun takes some minutes to reach its best rate and then some more to recover."

and the sort card, line 423, binned `light`:

> "A plant is moved from deep shade straight into full sunlight. For the first twenty minutes its rate of photosynthesis is lower than it will later settle at, and its chlorophyll fluorescence is low as well." — `data-why`: "...they are being shed as heat, deliberately, by an antenna the leaf has detuned."

What is wrong: the minutes-long lag on the way *into* the sun is photosynthetic induction, and its main limitation is the activation of rubisco, followed by the opening of the stomata; the light reactions are up within a minute or two. The antenna's detuning (non-photochemical quenching) engages within seconds to a minute; the cost it imposes is on the way *back into shade*, where it is slow to undo — the "some more to recover" half, which is the only half the sentence gets right. So the observation on the card is mostly an event in the stroma, its `data-why` names the wrong mechanism, and a reader who bins it under "the carbon reactions" has understood §6.6 and is marked wrong. This is the recipe's "an objective that tests something no section teaches" in its graded form.

Evidence: Taylor & Long, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 372:20160543 (2017), "Slow induction of photosynthesis on shade to sun transitions in wheat may cost at least 21% of productivity" — https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/372/1730/20160543/23327/Slow-induction-of-photosynthesis-on-shade-to-sun : induction has three phases — electron transport within seconds to 1–2 min, rubisco activation "generally completed within ~10 minutes", stomata 10–60 min — and "the major limitation during induction was activation of Rubisco, followed in importance by stomata". The slow relaxation of quenching on sun-to-shade transitions as the antenna-side cost: the Annual Review "Into the Shadows and Back into Sunlight: Photosynthesis in Fluctuating Light" — https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-arplant-070221-024745 .

Fix, prose: "...sheds energy as heat instead of passing it on. It switches on within a minute; it is slow to switch off, which is why a leaf moved from full sun back into shade works below its best efficiency for several minutes afterwards. The lag on the way *into* the sun is a different thing and belongs to Section 6.6: rubisco takes minutes to be switched on, and the stomata longer." Fix, card: rewrite it to the case the mechanism actually explains — "A leaf is moved from full sunlight into deep shade. For the next several minutes its photosynthesis per photon absorbed stays below what it will settle at, and its chlorophyll fluorescence stays low." — with a `data-why` about an antenna still detuned, contrasted with the herbicide card where fluorescence goes the other way. (Keeping the shade-to-sun observation and binning it `carbon` is also honest, but then the fluorescence clause must go.)

Severity: **error**. Objective: `diagnose-photosynthesis` — graded sort data changes; `antenna-and-protection` statement unchanged, but items must not present the induction lag as antenna or carotenoid behaviour.

### 3. Chapter 7 §7.5 (`chemiosmosis`) — the flagellar motor is not "this same machine"

### 8. Chapter 7 §7.3 — a fed body burns protein every day

`index.html` line 187:

> "A body burning protein for energy is a body that has run out of the other two, which is why it is a sign of starvation rather than of diet."

### 10. Chapter 6 §6.5 (`chemiosmosis`) — the three pH units are the isolated-thylakoid value, presented as the leaf's

### 11. Chapter 7 §7.5 — the "four per cent margin" prices cytosolic ATP with matrix protons, and §7.6 charges the missing proton one section later

`index.html` line 257: "Divide: 50 ÷ 19 is 2.6, so a little over two and a half protons must fall for every ATP made. The two sections price the same ATP with different protons, on consecutive screens, and the "tighter even than Section 5.4" comparison is made of the mismatch. The physical point §7.5 needs — that the synthase runs close enough to its balance point for a modest fall in the force to reverse it — is right and does not need the four per cent.

Evidence: the chapter's own §7.6 (line 305) and arithmetic: 3.7 × 19.3 = 71 kJ.

Fix: "Eight protons for three ATP is 2.7 each — just what the arithmetic asked for, and that is the point: the machine runs so close to its balance point that a modest fall in the force turns it round, which is where this section is going. (Section 7.6 adds the proton it costs to get the ATP out of the matrix and does the sum properly.)" Drop the comparison with §5.4's nine-tenths.

Severity: **weak**. Objective: `synthase-reversible`, `p-o-ratio` unchanged; items must not quote a four-per-cent margin.

**Chapter 6 — ship with the listed fixes.** Two errors, both in mechanism sentences that read as well as anything in the chapter: the six-carbon intermediate has been caught and characterised, and its instability is the enzyme's, not its own (finding 1); and the shade-to-sun lag belongs to rubisco and the stomata, not the antenna, which matters more than one sentence usually would because a graded card is built on it (finding 2). Below those, the chapter's habit of quoting textbook values as the leaf's — three pH units, an enzyme that "binds more tightly" — is the thing to watch (findings 9, 10), and one prerequisite points sideways (15). What the chapter does well it does very well: §6.3 is the best account of why a leaf is green I have read at this level, §6.4's "the hole is the point of the entire photosystem" is the right sentence, and §6.7's three-facts-as-one is a real argument. Its numbers are right everywhere I could check them, and they agree with chapter 5's to the digit.

**Chapter 7 — ship with the listed fixes, and it is the stronger of the two.** Six errors, but five of them are single clauses in §7.2–7.5 (the archaean glycolysis, "no other reason", protein and starvation, "this same machine", the peroxisome, the 1–2 %), each a mechanism stated one step further than the evidence goes; none touches the chapter's argument, which is the best in the book — the chain makes a gradient, the number is an efficiency, fermentation makes nothing, and what runs out is NAD⁺. The two things that would lift it are the seam and the sum: it opens as though chapter 6 had not just run the same machine (finding 13), and §7.5 prices the synthase's margin with §5.3's cytosolic ATP and the matrix's protons while §7.6, correctly, charges one more (finding 11). Fix the clauses, name chapter 6 once, and give the anaerobic table a control (finding 17), and it is the chapter the unit has been promising since Section 1.6.

Neither chapter can ship until it has an `items.js` and its eight figure modules, which is the gate's business and not mine.
