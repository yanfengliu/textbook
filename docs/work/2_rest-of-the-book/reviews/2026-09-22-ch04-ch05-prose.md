## Weak — true at textbook level, or true in part, and below the bar

### 4. Chapter 4 §4.4 explains osmosis by "more water per unit volume" and "bound water out of circulation", which the physics literature lists as the two standard misconceptions

**Location.** `biology/ch04-membranes-and-transport/index.html`, `<section id="osmosis">`, the paragraph opening on the term: "is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane … Water moves from where water is more concentrated to where it is less … There is simply more water, per unit of volume, on the side that is pure — and the sugar molecules, each with a shell of water held around it exactly as Section 2.3 described, take some of the rest out of circulation. Fewer free water molecules on the sugar side means fewer wandering back". Also the key idea ("Osmosis is diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane"), `glossary.js` `osmosis` ("The diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane … there are simply more free water molecules on one side than the other"), the objective statement `osmosis-mechanism` ("Explain osmosis as the diffusion of water, and say why adding a solute lowers the tendency of water to leave"), `i-osmosis-mechanism-1` (the correct option and the `why` on option 2), and `i-osmosis-mechanism-3` (rubric lines 2–4).

**What is wrong.** This is the Campbell/OpenStax account, and the book's bar is higher than theirs. Kramer and Myers' review of the thermodynamics re-derives osmosis from the canonical ensemble and names, as misconceptions found in published textbooks, (i) that osmosis is diffusion of water down a water concentration gradient — the water concentration difference for a dilute solution is of the wrong size to account for the effect, and osmotic flow through a pore is measurably bulk flow, not diffusion (osmotic permeability exceeds diffusional permeability); and (ii) that solute binds water and takes it out of circulation — which would make osmotic pressure depend on the solute's chemistry, when the chapter's own margin note says it is colligative ("count particles, not molecules"). The bound-water sentence and the particle-count margin note sit six lines apart and cannot both be the mechanism. The correct account is the one chapter 5 §5.2 will give the reader the tools for a chapter later: mixing has more arrangements, so ΔG of mixing is negative, water flows into the solution until the pressure term pays it back, and the flow is a pressure-driven flow of water, not a random walk.

**Evidence.** E. M. Kramer and D. R. Myers, "Five popular misconceptions about osmosis", *American Journal of Physics* 80, 694 (2012), https://pubs.aip.org/aapt/ajp/article/80/8/694/1057545; the same paper's summary at https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/518887 ("Osmosis is not driven by water dilution"). The chapter's own margin note on osmoles is the internal evidence against the bound-water sentence.

**Fix.** Everything quantitative in §4.4 — water potential, its two terms, the direction rule, the van 't Hoff numbers, tonicity — is right and stays. Replace the two mechanistic sentences with two others: "Nothing pulls the water. A solution has more arrangements available to it than pure water and pure solute side by side — the same accounting Section 5.2 will do for the hydrophobic effect — so water goes on entering the solution until the pressure it builds pays back what mixing gains." Say "net movement of water" rather than "diffusion of water" in the glossary, the key idea and the objective. Rewrite `i-osmosis-mechanism-1`'s correct option and option 2's `why`, and rubric lines 2–4 of `i-osmosis-mechanism-3`, to the same account.

**Severity: weak.** Touches: `osmosis-mechanism`; glossary `osmosis`; `i-osmosis-mechanism-1`, `i-osmosis-mechanism-3`.

### 6. Chapter 4 §4.6 — "A third of your resting metabolism goes on bailing out sodium" is above the sourced range

### 7. Chapter 5 §5.5 and three items repeat the "past about 40 °C the proteins of a human cell begin to unfold" claim that chapter 2 was corrected away from

## Verdict

**Chapter 4 — ship with the listed fixes.** It is the book's best chapter on continuity: the seam the last review found between chapters 2 and 3 is closed here, the numbers are right to the kilojoule, and §4.1, §4.5 and §4.7 argue rather than list. What must change before it is honest is small and local: one wrong plant in an alt text, a caption that may promise what its figure cannot do, a whole-body number half again its source, and an objective that asks for a model the section never names. The one thing that would lift it above the textbooks it was written from is finding 4 — osmosis told as the entropy of mixing, which the chapter after it will hand the reader the tools for anyway.

**Chapter 5 — ship with the listed fixes, and it is the stronger chapter of the two.** §5.3's ledger, §5.4's "nothing is paid for with heat" and §5.5's insistence that an enzyme changes when and never where are the clearest writing in the unit, and the item bank is the best in the book: distractors built from named misconceptions, arithmetic that checks against the figure's own numbers, and a header that says what the narrow composition drops. Its two errors are both sentences about the book rather than about biology — each says an earlier chapter left unexplained something that chapter explained — and both are one-line fixes; its one weak claim is the previous round's finding recurring a chapter later. Fix those, regroup the digits the way chapters 1 to 3 do, and cut the manual out of §5.6, and nothing in it is below the bar.
