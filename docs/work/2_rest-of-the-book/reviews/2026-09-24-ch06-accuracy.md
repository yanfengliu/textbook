## Findings

### 1. §6.3 (`pigments`), the four exits — "a photon slightly redder than the one absorbed" is true after red absorption and false after blue

Location: `index.html` line 167: "It can emit a photon slightly redder than the one it absorbed, which is fluorescence." Also, the same physics stated the same way: `glossary.js` `excited-state` — "it holds the photon's whole energy"; §6.1 margin note line 95 — "the energy leaves as heat or as a fainter photon" (a single photon has no brightness; the word wanted is *redder*, carrying less energy).

What is wrong: chlorophyll fluoresces from its lowest excited state whatever colour it absorbed. A red photon at 662 nm lands in that state directly, so the emitted photon, near 668 nm in a solvent and near 685 nm in a leaf, is indeed only slightly redder. A blue photon at 430 nm lands in a higher state, and the surplus over the red gap — about a third of the photon, 280 − 180 = 100 kJ/mol — is shed as heat within 100–250 femtoseconds, before any of the four exits is taken; the photon that then comes out is the same 668–685 nm one, some 250 nm redder than what went in, not slightly. The section itself has just spent a paragraph on the blue peak, so half the absorptions it describes contradict the sentence. The glossary's "holds the photon's whole energy" is the same claim from the other side: the state that does the chemistry holds a red photon's worth of energy whichever photon was absorbed.

Evidence: Kasha's rule — emission comes from the lowest excited singlet, so the emission wavelength is independent of the excitation wavelength (Kasha, *Discuss. Faraday Soc.* 9:14, 1950; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasha%27s_rule); the Soret-to-Q internal conversion in chlorophyll a measured at about 100–250 fs (reviewed in "Applying an anti-Kasha model resolves differences between photosynthetic and artificial pigments", *J. Phys. Chem. B* 2025, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12337091/); chlorophyll a fluorescence maxima at about 668 nm in ether and 685 nm in vivo at room temperature (Papageorgiou & Govindjee, eds., *Chlorophyll a Fluorescence: A Signature of Photosynthesis*, 2004). OpenStax does not cover fluorescence.

Why it matters beyond the sentence: it is the reason a blue photon and a red one are worth exactly one excitation each, which is what §6.4's photon arithmetic assumes and what `i-zscheme-arithmetic-3` (rubric point 6: "its surplus cannot pay for a second") and `i-photon-energy-3` (growers rate lamps by photons) already argue. Stating it here makes those two items follow from the prose instead of standing beside it.

Fix: "It can emit a photon, which is fluorescence — always at the red end of chlorophyll's spectrum, a little redder than its red absorption peak, whatever colour was absorbed: a blue photon's surplus over the red gap has already gone as heat, within a picosecond of absorption and before any exit is taken, which is why a blue photon and a red one each buy exactly one excitation." Glossary: "it holds a red photon's worth of energy — a blue photon's surplus is shed as heat within a picosecond". §6.1 margin: "as a redder photon".

Severity: **error** (false for the blue half of what the section describes; a few words to fix).

Touches: objective `antenna-and-protection` (statement unchanged); `excited-electron` (glossary entry); items `i-antenna-and-protection-2` (finding 9 below), `i-excited-electron-2` and `-3` (both already say only "redder" — no change), `i-zscheme-arithmetic-3` (unchanged; now supported by the prose).

### 2. §6.4 (`lightreactions`) — the section says the cluster releases oxygen only when four hits have accumulated, then that the first burst comes on the third flash, and never reconciles the two. It needs the clause.

Location: `index.html` line 208: "only when four have accumulated does it take four electrons off two waters at once and release the oxygen ... Nothing appears on the first two flashes, a burst comes on the third, then another on the seventh, another on the eleventh".

What is wrong: nothing in either sentence is false. Together they are a puzzle the section sets and does not solve: a reader who counts, as the section has just told them to, expects the fourth flash. The reconciliation exists on the page — the check `q3`'s explanation (line 483: "a dark-adapted cluster is mostly already one step along") — but a check is graded, not taught, and two items in the bank grade it too (`i-water-split-2` rubric point 7; `i-water-split-3`, whose whole point is that resetting the clusters moves the burst to the fourth flash). The objective `water-split` names §6.4 as what teaches it, so §6.4 has to say it. The figure builder's explanation is right, and its number is right: after minutes in the dark about three clusters in four rest one step along.

Evidence: Kok, Forbush & McGloin, *Photochem. Photobiol.* 11:457 (1970), the four-step model fitted to Joliot's flash yields with 25 % of centres in S0 and 75 % in S1 after dark adaptation; Styring & Rutherford, *Biochemistry* 26:2401 (1987), showing that in the dark the S0 state is slowly oxidised to S1 by the tyrosine-D radical, so a long-dark-adapted preparation is nearly all S1 — which is why the fraction "one step along" rises with the length of the dark and the first peak stays on the third flash. Joliot, Barbieri & Chabaud, *Photochem. Photobiol.* 10:309 (1969) for the pattern itself. OpenStax does not cover the flash experiment.

Fix, one clause: "Nothing appears on the first two flashes and a burst comes on the third — the third and not the fourth, because a cluster left in the dark settles one step along: its more oxidised states decay back, its fully reduced state is slowly oxidised by a tyrosine radical beside it, and so it starts the count at one — then another on the seventh, another on the eleventh, the peaks blurring as the population drifts out of step."

Severity: **weak** (true sentences that mislead together; the page grades the missing clause in one check and two items).

Touches: objective `water-split`; check `q3` (explanation already right); items `i-water-split-2`, `i-water-split-3` (both right, and both now taught by the prose); Figure 6.2's brief — see the figure notes at the end, because the by-hand mode ("only on the fourth") and the flash-train mode ("peaks at three") of the same figure need the same fact to agree on the stage.

### 3. §6.3 margin note — the accessory pigments were not "found by the gap between two curves before anyone had isolated them"

Location: `index.html` line 153: "Something else is absorbing there and passing the energy on, and that is the evidence for the accessory pigments — found by the gap between two curves before anyone had isolated them."

What is wrong: the order of history is reversed. Carotene was isolated from carrots by Wackenroder in 1831; Kützing isolated and named phycocyanin and phycoerythrin in 1843; Stokes showed spectroscopically in 1864 that "chlorophyll" is two green pigments, and Tswett separated them on his chromatography column in 1906. Engelmann's action spectra on diatoms, red algae and cyanobacteria (1883–84) came after all of the isolations but the last, and what they showed was not that the pigments existed but that light absorbed by known pigments other than chlorophyll drives oxygen production. The energy transfer from an accessory pigment to chlorophyll was proved later still — Dutton & Manning 1941 for fucoxanthin, Emerson & Lewis 1942 for phycocyanin. So the gap between the two curves found the pigments' *connection*, which is exactly what the sort card's `data-why` (line 413) says: "the observation that identifies which pigments are actually connected". The margin note contradicts the card.

Evidence: Wackenroder 1831 and the early history of carotene — https://www.chm.bris.ac.uk/motm/carotene/beta-carotene_history.html ; Kützing 1843 and the phycobiliproteins — Glazer's "Phycobiliproteins and phycobilisomes: the early observations", *Photosynth. Res.* 76:151 (2003), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1024954911473 ; Stokes 1864 and Tswett 1906 — the 1915 Nobel presentation for Willstätter, https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1915/press-release/ ; Engelmann's *Farbe und Assimilation* (1883) and the algae he used — Hintz, *Limnol. Oceanogr. Bull.* (2021), https://aslopubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/lob.10470 ; Dutton & Manning 1941 and Emerson & Lewis 1942 — Govindjee & Krogmann, "Discoveries in oxygenic photosynthesis (1727–2003)", *Photosynth. Res.* 80:15 (2004), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:PRES.0000030443.63979.e6 .

Fix: "...that is the evidence for the accessory pigments. The pigments themselves had been in bottles for decades — carotene since 1831 — but the gap between the two curves is what showed they were connected to the chemistry, and Engelmann drew it for diatoms and red algae within two years of his first experiment."

Severity: **error** (false history in an attribution the chapter chooses to make; no mechanism depends on it).

Touches: objective `absorption-vs-action` (statement unchanged — "explain what it means when the two do not match" — and its three items are right: `i-absorption-vs-action-1` says "another pigment is absorbing and passing the energy on"); the sort card is right as it stands.

### 7. `i-atmosphere-changed-3` — cyanobacteria do not pump carbon dioxide into carboxysomes

Location: `items.js` line 1839, the stem: "Cyanobacteria pump carbon dioxide into small protein shells, carboxysomes, where their rubisco works."

What is wrong: the pumps carry bicarbonate across the cell's membranes; a carbonic anhydrase inside the carboxysome turns it back into carbon dioxide beside rubisco, and the shell slows the gas's escape. The stem's mechanism is the one §6.8 has just spent a paragraph distinguishing from rubisco's (PEP carboxylase takes bicarbonate, and bicarbonate is what a cell can pump because it is charged). Evidence: Badger & Price, "CO2 concentrating mechanisms in cyanobacteria: molecular components, their diversity and evolution", *J. Exp. Bot.* 54:609 (2003).

Fix: "Cyanobacteria pump bicarbonate into the cell and release it as carbon dioxide inside small protein shells, carboxysomes, where their rubisco works." Severity: **small**. Touches: `atmosphere-changed`, `i-atmosphere-changed-3` (stem only).
